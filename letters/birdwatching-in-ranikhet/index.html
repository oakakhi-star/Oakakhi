Birdwatching in Ranikhet: the oak forest at dawn

From the Khalij pheasant to the blue whistling thrush, the forests around Ranikhet are alive with Himalayan birds. Where, when and what to look for.

The oak and pine forests around Ranikhet are among the most rewarding places in Kumaon for birdwatching. You do not need to travel far: from the terrace at Oakakhi, the morning chorus begins before the light does.

Birds you are likely to see

The forest around Oakakhi is home to many Himalayan species through the year. A few of the birds our guests most often see:

  • Khalij pheasant. Shy and beautiful, often seen at the forest edge in the early morning.
  • Blue whistling thrush. You will hear its long, human sounding whistle before you see it.
  • Red billed blue magpie. Long tailed, bold and unmistakable as it glides between trees.
  • Himalayan bulbul. Cheerful and common, with its swept back white cheeks.
  • Warblers and flycatchers, which change with the seasons.

The best time of day

First light, roughly between 5:30 and 8:00 in the morning, is when the forest is most active. Late afternoon is a quieter second window.

The best season

Spring, from March to May, brings breeding plumage and the most song. Winter is excellent too: the air is clear, the forest is open and some higher altitude birds come down into the oaks.

A dawn walk with Nishant

Nishant grew up listening to these forests and knows the songs before he sees the birds. A dawn session with him is two to three slow hours through the oak grove. Less a hike, more a listening session.

What to bring. Binoculars if you have them, soft coloured clothing, warm layers for the early hour and a little patience. A camera with a zoom lens helps, but is never essential.
